# Once a repo or area has one note there is no way to add another from the page

## Problem
A file page always offers a way to write a note about the file. A repo page and an area page offer one only while nobody has written anything yet, and the act of writing the first note is what takes the form away. So the web view invites a note from a reader who has nothing to say about the repo, and refuses one from the reader who has just read the page and does have something to say. The door closes at exactly the moment the repo starts accumulating knowledge, which is the moment the door starts to matter.

The mechanism is two different things that look alike. `fileAddNote` builds a real section — id `add-note`, heading "Add a note", no paragraphs, an action with `form: "note"` — and because the client renders an empty section's inline form, a section that is permanently empty is permanently a form. That section is listed in `fileStory` and nowhere else. `repoStory` and `areaStory` have no such section; what they have is an empty-state action on a section that carries content the rest of the time. The repo's "About this repo" section offers the form as its empty state, and it is empty only while the `_repo` note has no `why` entry. An area's "Read these first" offers the form as its empty state, and it is empty only while the note chain is empty.

The area case is the harsher of the two, because the chain an area reads is not its own. `noteChain` starts with the `_repo` note and then walks every ancestor folder before the area itself, so a single repo-level note fills the section for every area in the repo at once. `reggie onboard` writes a `_repo` note as part of setting a repo up. The practical effect is that from the first minute of a repo's life under Reggie, no area page anywhere has a note form, including an area with no note of its own that the page itself flags as having none: the read-first section can say in so many words that nothing is written on this folder and everything above is inherited, and then offer no way to write the missing thing.

This repo demonstrates it. Its `_repo` note carries a `why` entry and a `how` entry, so the repo page's "About this repo" is full and its form is gone, and every area page inherits that entry and has never shown a form at all.

Nothing is lost that cannot be recovered from a terminal — `reggie note add` takes any entity, and the MCP tool reaches the same function — but that is the shape of the complaint rather than a mitigation. The 2026-09-15 decisions ratify three doors for capture, the CLI verb, the MCP tool and the web form, and for notes the web door exists at file scope and half-exists above it. A reader who is on an area page precisely because they are trying to understand that area is the person most likely to have something worth recording about it, and they are the one told to go elsewhere.

The fix the intake line asks for is to emit the section for all three scopes rather than to rescue the empty states, which keeps the always-present form and the occasional empty state as two separate things and leaves the empty states alone. What that turns into is small: a scope-neutral version of `fileAddNote`, a place for it in two more section lists, empty text that does not say "this file", a per-scope command hint, and the same change written into the three places that record what a story contains — the exact section-id assertions in the tests, the section-ids-by-scope line in the API contract, and the spec. Two judgement calls sit inside it and neither is mechanical: where the section goes in the order, and what the form's default note type is, which on the repo page decides whether the note the reader just wrote appears under "About this repo" or under "How to run it".

## Why now
Notes are the substrate under most of what the web view claims to know. The story prose, the knowledge lens, the gap sections, the stale chips and the spotlight all read the same note store, and the graph can only ever say what is structurally true; everything about intent comes from what a person wrote down. Reggie's stated purpose is to help an owner keep understanding a codebase written faster than they can read it, and the half of that loop where understanding is written back down is the half with a missing button.

It is worth doing ahead of most of the backlog because it is the cheapest possible repair to that half. The form exists, the client already renders it for any empty section with a note action, the client already maps repo and area stories to their note entities, and the write endpoint takes any entity. Nothing has to be built; a section has to be emitted twice more and two decisions have to be made about it. That fits the small-items stretch the 2026-09-15 order places after the loop plumbing, and it is a good early exercise of the loop on work where the change is tiny and the reading around it is most of the job.

What gets worse while it waits is the gap between what the pages say and what they let you do. The knowledge lens is being pushed as the way to find where the repo is thin, and a lens that names a folder with no note of its own while that folder's page has no way to write one is an odd thing to promote. Pages are also gaining actions in general under the 2026-09-15 decisions, an idea action on every page for one, and adding a note is the older and simpler of the two writes; it would read badly for the newer action to arrive on every scope while the older one is still file-only.

## Suspected area
- `packages/reggie/src/story.ts` — the whole change. `fileAddNote` becomes scope-neutral and gains its place in `repoStory` and `areaStory`'s section lists; `EMPTY_TEXT.fileAddNote` loses its "about this file" wording or gains siblings; the command hint differs per scope, `_repo` for the repo and the trailing-slash directory entity for an area, matching what `reggie note add` accepts.
- `packages/reggie/src/story.ts`, the lens push — both `areaStory` and `fileStory` append their gaps section after the base list when the knowledge lens is on, and `fileStory` already puts `add-note` before that push. Where the new section goes in the repo and area lists has to hold that arrangement, not just look right in the default lens.
- `packages/reggie/src/story.test.ts` — three assertions spell out a scope's section ids as an exact array, one each for repo, area and file, and a fourth asserts that under the knowledge lens the area's last section is `gaps`. The first two change, the third does not, and the fourth is the constraint on placement. A test that the section is present and carries a form at every scope belongs with the change, since its absence above file scope is what went unnoticed.
- `packages/reggie/docs/ui-api-contract.md` — one line lists the section ids for every scope in order. It is the written record of the payload's shape, so it is wrong from the moment the payload changes and it is the thing a client author reads instead of the code.
- `packages/reggie/docs/ui-spec.md` — the repo-level table row describes the note form as part of the "About this repo" empty state, the file-level walkthrough names "Add a note" as a section of the file story, and the empty-state table has a row for the note sections. The spec has to say the section exists at all three scopes, or it keeps specifying the behaviour this task is removing.
- `packages/reggie/ui/story.js` — read first, then decide. `emptyBlock` renders the note form for any section whose action says `form: "note"`, and `noteEntityFor` already maps a repo story to `_repo`, an area story to its directory entity and a file story to its id, which is why the client needs nothing. The `HEADINGS` table is the exception: it is a second, hand-kept list of a scope's sections that drives the loading skeleton, so leaving it alone means the skeleton shows one set of headings and the payload another.
- `packages/reggie/ui/app.js` — the spotlight's add-note action and its fallback message, "Open a file or an area to add a note there", which is already untrue for areas. `focusNoteForm` takes the first `.form--note` in the document, which is worth knowing about on a page that could now carry two.
- `packages/reggie/src/serve.ts` — read only, to confirm the note endpoint scope-gates nothing and that a repo or area entity posted from the page lands where `reggie note add` would have put it.

## Open questions
- Where does the section sit in the repo order? After "How to run it" mirrors the file page, where it is last; near "About this repo" puts it beside the prose it feeds. The decisions of 2026-09-15 do not settle it and it changes the exact-array test either way.
- Where does it sit in the area order? Last, after "Recently", is the file page's arrangement; directly after "Read these first" puts writing a note next to reading the ones that exist.
- Does the knowledge lens keep `gaps` last at every scope? The file story already places `add-note` before the gaps push and an area test asserts `gaps` is last, so following the file's arrangement satisfies both; the question is whether that is the intent or an accident worth reversing while the order is open.
- What note type does the form default to at each scope? The file section defaults to `how` and the read-first empty state to `why`. On the repo page this is not cosmetic: `why` entries are the prose of "About this repo" and every other type is a card under "How to run it", so the default decides whether a reader's note appears where they were just reading.
- What is the empty text once it is scope-neutral? The current sentence names a file. One sentence for all three loses the chance to say what a repo note or an area note is for, and three sentences is three more strings to keep in the same register.
- Do the client's skeleton heading rows ride along or wait? They are a duplicate of the section list kept by hand in the client, and if they are left alone the loading state disagrees with the payload for the two changed scopes. The note that opened this task raises it and the 2026-09-15 decisions do not settle it.
- Does the spotlight's fallback message ride along or wait? It already claims a note can be added from an area page, which is false today and would become true; it would also still be silent about the repo page.
- What happens on a page that ends up with two note forms? A repo whose `_repo` note has no `why` entry, or an area in a repo with no `_repo` note at all, would render the empty state's form and the new section's form on the same page, and the spotlight's jump reaches whichever is first in the document. Either the empty states drop their forms above file scope, or the duplication is accepted and the jump target is chosen deliberately.
- Do the other scopes with a note entity get one too? The client already maps a workspace story to `_repo`, and services, flows and tasks have note entities of their own. Confining this to the three code scopes is defensible and should be a decision rather than an omission.
  > Answered by jacobpress on 2026-09-15: No. Repo, area and file only, per one repo at a time; the other pages get it when someone captures the ask.
- Does the heading stay "Add a note" everywhere, or name what is being written about? Three pages in a row headed identically read as the same control, which is either the point or a way to lose track of which entity the form is pointed at.

## Not this
- The client. It already renders the inline form for any empty section whose action asks for one, and it already maps a repo story and an area story to the right note entity, so the sections will work the moment the payload carries them. The two client files named above are read, and the skeleton rows and the spotlight message are the open question about whether anything there changes at all — not an assumption that it does.
- The empty states on "About this repo" and "Read these first". They keep offering the form when they are empty; this adds a section that is always there. Rescuing them instead, by making an area's read-first count only its own notes so it goes empty again, is a different change with a different cost, and it would make the read-first section lie about what a reader should read first.
- How notes are stored, resolved or typed. The note entity for a repo and for a directory, the note types, confidence and the staleness rules are all read here and none of them changes. If the entity spellings are awkward to type in a form that is worth capturing on its own.
- Editing, deleting or listing existing notes from the page, and any notes index. This is one more way in for a new note, nothing else.
- The reader drawer's line-range prefill. That is a file-scope surface with its own trigger and its own prefill text; it reaches the same form and is not changed by the form appearing elsewhere.
- The idea action that the 2026-09-15 decisions put on every page. That captures an intake line and opens a session; this writes a note. They will sit near each other on the same pages and are still separate work.
- The knowledge lens and its gaps sections. They are the reason the missing form is noticeable on area pages, and they are not edited here.
- `false-tests-sentence`, `graph-coverage-published` and the other small story-surface items captured on 2026-09-15. They share `story.ts` and nothing else; they touch its prose and this touches its section lists, so they should be sequenced for merge comfort rather than merged into one task.
